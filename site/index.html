§ 02 — Three pillars, one verdict

Your verdict is your worst pillar. Never the average.

A skill that is elegant, cheap, and insecure is not a B+. Averaging hides exactly the failure this tool exists to surface.

S

Safety

Injection patterns in tool descriptions, hidden Unicode and homoglyphs, credential-path reach, network egress, shell surface, provenance.

→ because 36% of scanned skills are flawed

C

Cost

Token weight of every tool schema and skill body, the tax they impose per turn, your share of window consumed, projected spend.

→ because one server can eat 21% of your window

Q

Quality

Structure, trigger clarity, length against usefulness, and semantic overlap with the other skills you already have installed.

→ because curation is worth +16.2 points of pass rate

§ 03 — The part nobody else does

Every scanner is blind to time.

A scanner tells you a thing looks fine today. ClawHavoc poisoned 1,184 skills through updates — the attack that actually happened is a change over time.

cupel diff keeps a lockfile and reports what changed. Rug-pull detection, running locally, on your machine.

  + egress api.telemetry-collect.net   (new)

§ 04 — Non-negotiable

No network.

Zero calls in the default path — enforced by test, not by promise. A tool that audits trust cannot itself be a data-collection channel.

No account. No telemetry.

Nothing leaves your machine. Ever. Credential values in your config files are never read, never stored, never rendered.

Cross-platform.

Existing runtime tools are Linux-kernel-only — Landlock, seccomp — or enterprise platforms. Cupel runs on your Mac.

Rules are data.

Detection rules are versioned YAML with their own test cases. Contribute one without reading a line of TypeScript.

A cupel is the vessel that reveals what a metal is actually made of

Heat it. See what survives.
